## Impact of US Tariff Changes on Private Credit Sectors

### Introduction

The private credit sector, which has shown significant growth over recent years, faces new challenges under the changing US tariff regime as of 2026. ### Overview of 2026 Tariff Changes

**Tariff Landscape**: President Trump's administration has been pivotal in reshaping tariffs in 2026, moving from the now-defunct International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) tariffs, which were invalidated by a Supreme Court ruling, to imposing Section 122 tariffs. These changes have led to uncertainty but also a lower average effective tariff rate compared to previous years.

**Key Tariff Developments**:
- **IEEPA Tariffs**: Struck down by the Supreme Court, these tariffs had a significant historical impact on federal revenue and market conditions.
- **Section 122 Tariffs**: Implemented to maintain tariff revenue, these are time-limited unless extended by Congress and cover a vast range of imports, essentially altering prior economic forecasts.

### Impact on Private Credit Sectors

**Private Credit Growth and Challenges**: The private credit market, currently valued at approximately $3 trillion, does not operate in isolation from these macroeconomic changes. Tariff adjustments impact leverage, covenant quality, and the overall financial health of underlying borrowers.

3. **NAV Facilities and Subscription Credit Lines**:

4. **Preferred Equity**:
